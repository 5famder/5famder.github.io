01 · The challenge

AI moves faster than a course catalogue

In a consulting firm, "AI skills" mean different things for a data engineer, a risk advisor and a deals analyst. A single generic AI course creates awareness but rarely changes how people deliver client work. The learning offering needs to be role-specific, tied to real tasks, and refreshed as tools and regulation change.

1

Different roles, different gaps

Engineers need evaluation and agent design; risk advisors need regulation and output verification; leaders need to scope and price AI-enabled work.

2

Knowing ≠ using

Completion rates say little. What matters is whether people use approved AI tools safely on billable work and improve quality or speed.

3

Content goes stale

Tool-specific modules age in months. The portfolio needs a regular keep / refresh / retire rhythm, plus an AI-literacy duty for staff under the EU AI Act (Art. 4).

02 · Approach

Four connected steps

Diagnose

Skills map

Define 8 AI skill domains and target levels per role. Validate with practice leads and a pilot self-assessment.

Design

Role journeys

Turn the biggest gaps into short, task-based journeys: learn, apply on own work, get feedback, show evidence.

Engage

Adoption

AI Advocates in each team, show-and-tell sessions, a shared prompt and use-case library.

Measure

Impact & currency

Track proficiency, tool adoption and task impact; review the portfolio quarterly with a steering group.

04 · Deliverable 2

Role-based learning journeys

Every journey follows the same rhythm (diagnose, learn, apply on real work, deepen, show evidence), so it is easy to maintain, while content and practice tasks are specific to the role. Roughly 2 hours a week, mostly in the flow of work.

05 · Engagement

Making AI learning stick in daily work

AI Advocates

One advocate per team: early access to new tools, a monthly huddle with L&D, and a role in coaching journey participants and flagging adoption barriers.

"AI in 15"

Fortnightly 15-minute show-and-tell where a consultant demos one real use case. Recorded, tagged by role, added to the library.

Use-case & prompt library

Every journey ends with a use case that goes into a shared library, so learning output becomes reusable practice for the next cohort.

06 · Governance & impact

What the steering group sees

1Reach

  • Enrolment and progress by role and unit
  • Coverage of AI-literacy baseline

2Proficiency

  • Pre/post level against the skills map
  • Scenario scores, SME-reviewed evidence

3Adoption

  • Active use of approved AI tools
  • Use cases added to the library

4Impact

  • Time saved on target tasks
  • Quality of reviewed deliverables
  • Manager and client feedback

Quarterly portfolio triage

Keep

  • Proficiency gains visible
  • Content checked in last 6 months

Refresh

  • Tool or regulation changed
  • Learners report outdated examples

Retire

  • Low use and no skill link
  • Duplicated by a better offer

Build or adopt

  • New gap in the skills map
  • Check Global / EMEA offer before building locally
07 · How I would start

First 90 days

Days 1–30 · Listen
  • Interview practice leads and AI advocates in T&T and SR&T
  • Audit the current AI portfolio and Global / EMEA offer
  • Draft the skills map with real role titles
Days 31–60 · Prioritise
  • Validate the map with a pilot self-assessment
  • Rank gaps by business priority
  • Agree roadmap and dashboard with the steering group
Days 61–90 · Pilot
  • Run one role journey with a small cohort
  • Measure proficiency and adoption
  • Bring results and next choices to the steering group
