About this project

The challenge

A new service technician may know cars well but have never worked near a high-voltage system. Before they work on an EV alone, the business needs proof that they can do it safely, not just a completed course.

My approach

I started with the job, not the content. I broke safe HV work down into what a technician must actually do and turned it into a competency map: 6 domains, 18 competencies, each with an observable criterion, a target level and an assessment method.

Key design decisions

  • Doing over knowing. Three proficiency levels show which skills need only understanding and which must be proven by hand.
  • Safety-critical gates. 9 competencies need a zero-error pass, so a high quiz score can't hide a dangerous gap.
  • Yes/no criteria. Each criterion can be ticked by a mentor on the shop floor, which keeps the practical fair across sites.
  • Consequences, not "incorrect". Abnormal situations are taught through a branching scenario that shows what would really happen.

Deliverables

Competency map, certification path design, and this visual road map for learners and managers.

Tools

Task analysis and competency-based assessment design. Planned build: Articulate Storyline, HeyGen, LMS.

Next steps

Validate procedures against the OEM service manual with a subject matter expert, then pilot the practical checklist with two mentors.
